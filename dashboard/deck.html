How it works

Output: a candidate timing plan for an engineer to review.

The simulator is like a video game of Stevens Creek. The tuner changes the lights, watches the score, and learns which settings to try next.

The tuner learns from its own tries

Try 0 was textbook. Fresh runs picked the finalist.

Bayesian optimization learns from its own trials. It is not a neural network. Each trial runs 3 practice rush hours. Try 0 scored 466 seconds per car, and selected try 39 scored 287 on practice.

What it can change, and how we score it

The tuner changes 32 settings. The score counts every requested car. Mean side-street, eastbound and westbound results must stay within 5% of textbook. Every seed must have at most 2 extra stuck-car events. Rush hour passes these aggregate rules, night does not.

What we would do next
