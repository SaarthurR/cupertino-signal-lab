How we built it

We rebuilt the real street in a traffic simulator, filled it with cars from the city counts, built the plan an engineer would use, then let a computer search for a better one.

Scored against rules we wrote down before the test. It won the number we promised to judge on, and failed a rule we set ourselves.

Our plan cut the time an average car loses by about 25 percent across 20 rush hours it had never seen, and it was better in 17 of the 20. But westbound drivers lost 38 percent, and on 8 runs it broke our own rule about leaving cars stuck. So: promising, not ready.

What we would do next

  • Fix the westbound timing. That is the failure.
  • Ask VTA for counts newer than 2018.
  • Put pedestrians and bikes in the model.
  • Try a second corridor.
